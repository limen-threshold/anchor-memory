# v1.22.0 — Sinking is not deleting; the old-memory floor may not evict a literal hit

[中文说明](v1.22.0_zh.md)

Two small changes to what `search()` and `dream_pass()` are allowed to throw away. No API changes; two new knobs on `AnchorMemory`, both with the old behaviour one assignment away.

## 1. Short-tier memories are no longer deleted by default

Through v1.21 a `short`-tier memory older than 14 days was removed by `decay_short()` (and by `dream_pass()`, which calls it) — archived to `deleted_memories.jsonl` first since v1.18, but gone from both stores. From v1.22:

- `decay_short()` is a no-op unless asked. `AnchorMemory.short_delete` defaults to `False`; `decay_short(days, delete=True)` or `short_delete = True` restores the 14-day deletion exactly as before (archive first, both stores).
- Nothing else changes for those rows: no down-weighting, no floor, no new score term. They sink the way every memory already sinks — through the recency boost fading to zero — and stay searchable.
- `AnchorDB.decay_short()` (the SQLite-only method) is untouched; it was never the recommended entry point.

Why: in the upstream deployment this library came from, the deletion step was the only thing in the dream pass that lost data and the only step whose mistakes were discovered weeks later (a timestamp repair in September pushed twenty fragments over the 14-day line in one night). Forgetting-as-deletion bought nothing that forgetting-as-sinking does not, and cost the ability to look back. The tier name stays so that existing stores and callers keep working.

## 2. The old-memory floor may not evict a keyword-lane hit

`_allocate_slots` keeps at least `older_reserve` memories older than `older_days` in the final top-N; when the top-N is full of young rows it swaps the worst-scoring young row out for the best old one. Upstream replay (2026-10-22) caught that swap removing two *literal* matches — rows the keyword lane had found by the exact words of the query, scoring 0.10 — in favour of two old vector hits scoring 0.42 that answered nothing.

- Rows with `_debug_source == "keyword"` are never chosen as the victim. `AnchorMemory.protect_literal = False` restores the v1.14–v1.21 behaviour.
- On a tie between two young rows the later one (lower rank) goes; before, `max()` picked the earlier one.
- The upstream fix also refuses the swap when the old row's score is not better by a margin (0.25 there). That part is **not** ported: the margin was calibrated on that deployment's score distribution and would not transfer across embedders.

## Benchmark note

LongMemEval-S (`benchmarks/longmemeval/`) stores every haystack turn with its 2023 timestamp, so on that data every row is "old" by the floor's definition and the old-memory floor never fires — neither change can move the score. A 40-question sanity run on v1.22 reproduced the v1.21 lanes question for question (`benchmarks/longmemeval/runs_v121/` keeps the v1.21 summary; the per-question files stay local, as the benchmark's `.gitignore` says). The floor is covered by `tests/smoke_v122.py`; the deletion switch by `tests/smoke_v122.py` and the (now opt-in) path in `tests/smoke_v118.py`.

## Upgrading

Nothing to migrate. If you relied on `dream_pass()` to prune `short` rows, set `mem.short_delete = True` once after construction.
